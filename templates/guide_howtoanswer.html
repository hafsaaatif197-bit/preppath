How to Answer Questions

Knowing what to say is only half the battle. How you structure and deliver your answers is what separates good candidates from great ones.

The STAR Method

STAR is the most widely recommended framework for answering behavioural interview questions. It gives your answer a clear structure that is easy to follow and demonstrates your thinking.

S
SituationSet the scene. Where were you, what was the context?
T
TaskWhat was your specific responsibility in that situation?
A
ActionWhat did YOU do? Be specific — use "I", not "we".
R
ResultWhat happened? Quantify where possible.

Example STAR Answer

Question: "Tell me about a time you worked under pressure."

  • S — During my final year, our group project lost a team member two weeks before the deadline.
  • T — I was responsible for redistributing the work and keeping the team motivated under a tight timeline.
  • A — I created a shared task board, held daily ten-minute check-ins, and took on the most critical missing sections myself.
  • R — We submitted on time and received a grade of 74%, which was above our previous average for the module.

General Tips for Any Question

  • Pause for two seconds before answering. This is not weakness — it shows you are thoughtful.
  • Keep answers between 90 seconds and 2 minutes. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to be engaging.
  • Always end on the result or what you learned. Never leave an answer trailing off.
  • Avoid filler words like "um", "like", and "basically". Practise until they disappear.
  • If you do not understand a question, ask for clarification — this is professional, not a weakness.
