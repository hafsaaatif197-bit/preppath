Dressing & Presentation

You are assessed before you say a single word. How you dress communicates professionalism, preparation, and respect.

The Golden Rule

Always dress one level above the company's everyday dress code. If they are business casual, go smart casual. If they are casual, go business casual. When in doubt, it is always safer to be slightly overdressed than underdressed. You can always dress down; you cannot undo a poor first impression.

By Industry

  • Finance & Law: Full business formal. Dark suit, white or light shirt, conservative tie for men. Tailored suit or formal dress for women.
  • Tech & Startups: Smart casual. Clean chinos or dark jeans, a neat shirt or blouse. Avoid gym wear or anything too casual.
  • Creative Industries: Smart with personality. You can show individual style but keep it polished and intentional.
  • Healthcare & Education: Conservative and neat. Modest, clean, professional — nothing that would distract or create discomfort.

Presentation Checklist

  • Clothes are clean, ironed, and well-fitted — not too tight or too loose.
  • Shoes are clean and appropriate for the outfit.
  • Hair is neat and groomed. If long, consider whether it should be tied back.
  • Minimal and subtle fragrance — strong perfume or cologne can be off-putting in a small room.
  • Nails are clean and tidy.
  • Bag or portfolio looks professional, not worn out.
